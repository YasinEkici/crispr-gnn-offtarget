# Genome-Wide Binding of the CRISPR Endonuclease Cas9 in Mammalian Cells

## Citation

Wu, X., Scott, D. A., Kriz, A. J., Chiu, A. C., Hsu, P. D., Dadon, D. B., Cheng, A. W., Trevino, A. E., Konermann, S., Tsai, S. Q., Delebecque, N. J., Niemi, D. M., Long, C., Jaenisch, R., Zhang, F., & Sharp, P. A. (2014). Genome-wide binding of the CRISPR endonuclease Cas9 in mammalian cells. *Nature Biotechnology*, 32(7), 670-676. https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.2889

## Core Idea

This study used genome-wide binding assays to characterize Cas9/dCas9 binding in mammalian cells. It helped establish that CRISPR targeting behavior is not just a local sequence-matching problem; genome context and chromatin-related accessibility can influence binding landscapes.

## Project Relevance

This supports the biological framing behind Sprint 5 and Sprint 7E/7F: epigenetic and chromatin-context descriptors can carry real signal for CRISPR activity modeling.

## Project Difference

The project predicts measured off-target cleavage under its frozen guide-disjoint evaluation contract. It does not reproduce a dCas9 ChIP-seq binding assay.

## Claim Boundary

Use as biological motivation for chromatin-context features. Do not treat it as proof that a specific feature in this project is causal.
