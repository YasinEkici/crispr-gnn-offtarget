#### **Abstract**

The CRISPR (clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats)/Cas (CRISPR-associated protein) programmable nuclease system continues to evolve, with in vivo therapeutic gene editing increasingly applied in clinical settings. However, off-target effects remain a significant challenge, hindering its broader clinical application. To enhance the development of gene-editing therapies and the accuracy of prediction algorithms, we developed CRISPRoffT [\(https://ccsm.uth.edu/CRISPRoffT/\)](https://ccsm.uth.edu/CRISPRoffT/). Users can access a comprehensive repository of off-target regions predicted and validated by a diverse range of technologies across various cell lines, Cas enzyme variants, engineered sgRNAs (single guide RNAs) and CRISPR editing systems. CRISPRoffT integrates results of off-target analysis from 74 studies, encompassing 29 experimental prediction techniques, 368 guide sequences, 226 164 potential guide and off-target pairs and 8840 validated off-targets. CRISPRoffT features off-target data from different CRISPR approaches (knockout, base editing and prime editing) applied under diverse experimental conditions, including 85 different Cas/guide RNA (gRNA) combinations used across 34 different human and mouse cell lines. CRISPRoffT provides results of comparative analyses for individual guide sequences, genes, cell types, techniques and Cas/gRNA combinations under different conditions. CRISPRoffT is a unique resource providing valuable insights that facilitate the safety-driven design of CRISPR-based therapeutics, inform experimental design, advance the development of computational off-target prediction algorithms and guide RNA design algorithms.

# **Introduction**

CRISPR (clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats)/Cas (CRISPR-associated protein) gene editing is a cutting-edge technology that allows for the precise modification of specific loci within an organism's genome. Currently, there are three main types of CRISPR/Cas systems: knockout system, prime editing system and base editing system. This system consists of two key components: the Cas endonuclease and a guide RNA (gRNA). The gRNA is complimentary to a specific DNA sequence, guiding the Cas protein to an exact location in the genome where modification is desired [\(1\)](#page-8-0) (Figure [1A](#page-1-0)). and its potential is increasingly recognized in clinical applications. Recently, the first CRISPR/Cas9 gene-editing therapy, Casgevy, was approved for the treatment of sicklecell disease in patients 12 years and older with recurrent vaso-occlusive crises (https://www.fda.gov/news-events/ [press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapies](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapies-treat-patients-sickle-cell-disease)treat-patients-sickle-cell-disease). CRISPR/Cas technology also shows promise in personalized cancer treatment such as genetically modified chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy [\(2\)](#page-9-0), as well as in addressing genetic diseases and infectious diseases [\(3\)](#page-9-0). As of 15 August 2024, there are 83 clinical studies related to CRISPR registered at [http://clinicaltrials.gov.](http://clinicaltrials.gov)

Despite its precision, CRISPR/Cas nucleases carry the risk of unintended edits at off-target locations, which remains a significant concern in the development of CRISPR-based therapies (Figure 1B). Off-target activity can have severe consequences, as it may inadvertently alter gene expression [\(4\)](#page-9-0). Currently, several prediction algorithms [\(5–10\)](#page-9-0) and experimental technologies [\(11–33\)](#page-9-0) exist to predict off-target effects, along with guide sequence design algorithms [\(34–43\)](#page-9-0) that are designed to minimize off-target risks. However, these methods have not yet been shown to reliably predict all possible offtargets for a particular guide sequence. A key challenge is the lack of a comprehensive resource of validated off-target data. The creation of such a resource is hindered by the laborintensive process of manually collating target sequences from the literature, as off-target data are often scattered in the supplementary materials of various publications. Therefore, this situation highlights the critical need for the systematic collection of off-target data, which is crucial for improving CRISPR/Cas off-target prediction algorithms, refining gRNA design, validating novel off-target assays [\(44\)](#page-10-0) and ultimately leading to safer and more effective CRISPR-based gene-editing therapies that can better address human diseases.

To date, there exists only one database of CRISPR offtargets, crisprSQL, which provides experimentally identified off-targets for only the SpCas9 enzyme [\(45\)](#page-10-0). However, this database is limited, as it compiles off-target data from only 17 previous studies and 10 technologies. Some CRISPR gRNA design or off-target prediction algorithms, such as CRISPOR [\(37\)](#page-9-0) and DeepCRISPR [\(34\)](#page-9-0), also provide off-target data from a limited number of publications. Currently, there is no comprehensive and up-to-date resource that includes both validated and predicted off-target locations. To address this need, we developed CRISPRoffT, a comprehensive database that compiles both experimentally predicted and validated offtargets from across various technologies, human and mouse cell types, cell lines, tissues, Cas enzyme variants, engineered

sgRNAs and CRISPR editing systems. CRISPRoffT delivers important insights into the safety of a wide range of CRISPR editing systems with therapeutic potential, as well as a valuable training resource for algorithm development.

To accomplish this, we conducted an extensive literature search and manually curated predicted and validated offtarget data from a total of 74 studies. These studies covered 29 experimental prediction technologies, 368 guide sequences, 226 164 potential guide and off-target pairs and 8840 validated off-targets. Cas enzymes, delivery modes [\(46\)](#page-10-0) and epigenetic factors [\(47\)](#page-10-0) have been reported to be associated with the cleavage of the CRISPR system. Therefore, we not only collected different kinds of engineered Cas enzymes, detailed experimental information, but also annotated the epigenetic factors of each target site. In addition, our database includes comparative analyses of each guide sequence, target gene, cell type, prediction technology and Cas/gRNA combination under various conditions. In summary, CRISPRoffT provides comprehensive knowledge that enhances clinical, laboratory, and computational research related to CRISPR/Cas gene editing.

# **Materials and methods**

#### Data collection

To provide comprehensive coverage of experimentally predicted and validated off-targets, we queried PubMed Central [\(](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)<https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/>[\)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and PubMed (https: //pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) using search terms that included names of experimental off-target prediction methods, such as 'GUIDE-seq AND off-target' and 'CIRCLE-seq AND offtarget'. We included publications that provide experimentally predicted off-target sequences for guides with clear on-target activity. Pure on-target studies, gene knockout screen studies and studies without off-target analysis were thereby excluded. Our search resulted in the identification of 74 articles that utilized 29 different experimental methods [\(Supplementary](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article-lookup/doi/10.1093/nar/gkae1025#supplementary-data) Table S1).

#### Data processing

Off-target prediction results are often presented in PDF format as charts that align off-target sequences with the guide sequence, typically showing the predicted cleavage rate or score alongside each off-target. For such cases, off-target sequences and cleavage rates were extracted either manually or using various PDF-editing tools. For each guide sequence, the ontarget sequence, location and gene were obtained. Off-target sequences with missing or incorrect genomic coordinates (the positions of the first and last bases) were aligned to a speciesappropriate reference genome using Bowtie1 [\(48\)](#page-10-0) and annotated with accurate coordinates. For targets presented without the PAM (protospacer adjacent motif) sequence, we expanded the genomic location of the targetsequence to include adjacent bases falling within the nuclease-specific PAM region given by the original article. Then, the bedtools [\(49\)](#page-10-0) *getfasta* function was used to retrieve the corresponding PAM sequence. To annotate targets with the appropriate gene name, we retrieved the relevant genome fasta files from the GENCODE [\(50\)](#page-10-0) and Ensembl [\(51\)](#page-10-0) databases, extracted the gene annotations and converted them to bed format. The bedtools *intersect* function was then employed to annotate gene symbols based on genomic location.

# Annotation of validated off-targets

If original studies chose to perform experimental validation of predicted off-targets through targeted deep sequencing, offtargets were annotated as either 'TRUE' or 'FALSE' based on whether a significant level of editing, as reported in the original paper, occurred at that locus compared with untreated controls. The indel rate (treated–control) was also extracted where available. For validation data, the choice of targeted deep sequencing technology can strongly affect the ability to validate off-targets with low nuclease activity levels [\(52\)](#page-10-0),so all validation data were annotated with the specific targeted deep sequencing technology used. Additionally,since validation experiments are typically performed under different conditions than prediction experiments to better reflect *in vivo* treatment scenarios, the specific conditions for targeted deep sequencing were provided.

#### Annotation of epigenetic factors

It was reported that epigenetic factors may influence the cleavage activity of CRISPR/Cas systems in eukaryotic cells [\(47,53,54\)](#page-10-0). To explore the association between epigenetic factors and the editing efficiency of CRISPR/Cas systems, epigenetic factors for each site were annotated based on their genomic loci. The ENCODE database [\(55\)](#page-10-0) provides a platform that stores epigenetic experimental data. SCREEN [\(56\)](#page-10-0) is a search tool for the ENCODE database, summarizing the presence of certain epigenetic factors and candidate regulatory elements across the human (hg38) and mouse (mm10) genomes for various cell lines or tissues. The bed file was obtained from the SCREEN web interface and contains the genomic regions where the three epigenetic markers are located: (i) CTCF: CCCTC-binding factor, found at sites that activate gene transcription and are involved in chromatin organization [\(57\)](#page-10-0), (ii) DNase-seq: sites sensitive to the DNase I enzyme correspond to open chromatin, indicating the accessibility of the DNA strand [\(58\)](#page-10-0) and (iii) H3K4me3: trimethylation of lysine-4 on the histone H3 protein, associated with the transcription of nearby genes [\(59\)](#page-10-0). RRBS data (hg19) was obtained from the ENCODE database and used to evaluate the DNA methylation state by converting unmethylated cytosines to uracil [\(60\)](#page-10-0). These epigenetic factors have become a standard in computational off-target prediction [\(34](#page-9-0)[,61\)](#page-10-0). Annotation was performed using the bedtools *intersect* command. The annotation of epigenetic markers in different cell lines can be found in [Supplementary](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article-lookup/doi/10.1093/nar/gkae1025#supplementary-data) Table S2.

# Other information included in the database

Highly efficient Cas-variants, engineered sgRNAs and base or prime editors could significantly reduce off-target effects [\(4,](#page-9-0)[62\)](#page-10-0), making it necessary to include nuclease and sgRNA types alongside each guide. In addition, various experimental conditions can greatly influence off-target activity. Factors, such as whether the nuclease and guide are delivered as RNPs (ribonucleoprotein complexes) or plasmids, the method of delivery (e.g. electroporation, lipofection and viral) and incubation time, all have demonstrable effects on off-target activity [\(4\)](#page-9-0), and these details are contained in the database. For prediction technologies used in cell lines, performing predictions across multiple cell lines helps to evaluate the performance of these technologies. As a result, several studies have conducted off-target analyses of the same guides under different experimental conditions line (e.g. HEK293, U2OS, K562, etc.). Therefore, experimental conditions were included whenever possible, to differentiate between separate analyses of the same guide. For cell-free technologies performed on isolated genetic DNA, data were annotated with the original cellular source of the DNA, while species and organ systems were provided for *in vivo* technologies.

#### Comparative analysis and statistics

In addition to providing potential and validated off-target data, our database also provides detailed overlap statistics for each guide sequence across different technologies and conditions, presented by Venn plots and UpSet plots. Off-target validation information is presented using dot plots, allowing for a clear comparison of the status of different on-target sites for each gene under varying technologies and conditions. Furthermore, we compared the number of potential off-targets and the ratio of validated off-targets across different cell lines (cell types or tissues), technologies and Cas/gRNA combinations. These visualizations, including the Venn plots, UpSet plots and dot plots, were generated using R (version 4.3.2).

#### Database architecture

The CRISPRoffT system is built on a three-tier architecture consisting of the client, server and database layers. It features a user-friendly web interface, a Perl's DBI (Perl Database Interface) module and a MySQL database. This database was developed using MySQL 3.23 with the MyISAM storage engine.

# **Results**

# Overview of CRISPRoffT

CRISPRoffT provides users with a comprehensive collection of experimentally predicted CRISPR off-targets curated from publicly available articles, along with their validation status and comparative analysis under different conditions. After a thorough literature search and selection process, we manually collected and processed results of off-target analysis from supplementary files, including experimentally predicted and validated off-targets. To ensure accuracy, we used tools such as Bowtie, Bedtools and UCSC BLAT [\(63\)](#page-10-0) to identify accurate genomic information and conducted a series of comparative analyses, ultimately constructing the CRISORoffT database (Figure [2\)](#page-4-0). The main functional modules of CRISPRoffT include 'Search' and 'Browse'. The 'Search' function allows users to query by guide sequence, gene symbol, target sequence and target region. The 'Browse' function enables exploration by source, technology and Cas/gRNA combination. Each search or browse action provides access to detailed annotated pages. Additionally, all data and analysis results are available for download and are visually presented through components such as Venn plots, UpSet plots and dot plots.

#### Data statistics of CRISPRoffT

In CRISPRoffT, we searched the relevant literature and manually collected predicted and validated off-target data from a total of 74 studies covering 29 experimental prediction technologies (Figure [3A](#page-5-0) and D). Of these off-target prediction technologies, 10 are conducted on cell-free genomic DNA, 15 in cells and 5 *in vivo*. This database includes 368 guide sequences, with a total of 226 164 potential guide and off-target pairs associated with 22 632 genes. Out of these, 8840 offtargets were experimentally validated, with 1844 confirmed as true off-targets and 6996 determined as false off-targets. A total of 34 different cell lines, cell types or tissues from *Homo sapiens* (10 cell lines and 12 cell types) and *Mus musculus* (4 cell lines, 5 cell types and 3 tissues) were used for offtarget prediction (Figure [3B](#page-5-0)). CRISPRoffT includes 85 combinations of Cas enzyme variants and engineered sgRNAs, comprising 39 Cas9 enzymes, 10 Cas12a enzymes, 24 prime editors and 14 base editors (Figure [3C](#page-5-0)). Three different CRISPR editing systems were included: the knockout system (Cas9 and Cas12a), the prime editing system (prime editor) and the base editing system (base editor). Prediction technologies varied greatly in the average number of off-targets predicted per ontarget (Figure [3E](#page-5-0)).

#### CRISPRoffT search and browse

Users can perform searches by guide sequence, gene name, target sequence and target region (Figure [4A](#page-6-0)). The length of guide and target sequences varies depending on the Cas enzyme and the sgRNA used. For example, the guide sequence for wild-type Cas9 is 23 nucleotides long, whereas for wildtype Cpf1 (Cas12a), it is 24 nucleotides long. To search for a gene in *Homo sapiens*, first select *'Homo sapiens*' and then enter the correct gene symbol, such as 'FANCF'. Similarly, for *Mus musculus*,symbols like 'Pcsk9' should be used. For target region searches, the appropriate genome assembly must first be selected. When searching for a guide sequence, the website will display off-targets associated with that guide sequence and related analysis results (Figure [4B](#page-6-0)). When searching for a gene, the website will show both on-target sites and off-target sites related to this gene, along with related analyses (Figure [4C](#page-6-0)). When searching for a target sequence, the website will show related on-target and off-target sites (Figure [4D](#page-6-0)). When searching for a target region, the website will display all ontarget and off-target sites within that region (Figure [4E](#page-6-0)). Additionally, users can browse by source (cell line, cell type or tissue), technology and Cas/gRNA combination (Figure [5A](#page-7-0)), and access detailed off-target information and comparative analyses under this browse category (Figure [5B](#page-7-0)).

# **Annotation page by browsing source, technology and Cas***/***gRNA combination**

On the browse annotation page (Figure 5B), CRISPRoffT begins with a brief description of the cell line, technology or Cas enzyme (Figure 5B-i). Then, a summary table of on-target sites is displayed, including the guide sequence, target region, assembly genome, on-target gene site, gene name and identity (on- or off-target) (Figure [5B](#page-7-0)-ii). Next, CRISPRoffT offers a comparative analysis of the potential off-target counts and the validated off-target ratios for different on-target sites within the selected browse category, represented through a dot plot (Figure [5B](#page-7-0)-iii). From the analysis results, we found that most experiments do not validate all predicted potential off-targets, and true off-targets account for <50% of the validated off-targets (Figure [5B](#page-7-0)-iii). These findings underscore the necessity for developing more precise technologies.

# **Discussion**

We present CRISPRoffT, a web-based public resource that integrates an expansive amount of experimentally predicted and validated off-target data across different technologies, human and mouse cell types, Cas enzyme variants, engineered sgRNA, and CRISPR editing systems. This database also provides comparative analyses between different experimental conditions for each guide sequence, gene, cell type, technology and Cas/gRNA combination. To the best of our knowledge, CRISPRoffT represents the largest scale repository of validated off-target data generated by the latest off-target prediction technologies, alongside corresponding experimental data. Overall, CRISPRoffT exhibits several innovative features: (i) The current version of CRISPRoffT curates and organizes 226 164 potential guide and off-target pairs from state-of-theart or widely used technologies. (ii) CRISPRoffT provides the largest amount of validated off-target data of any database to date, with a total of 8840 off-targets, which could be used for off-target prediction algorithms and gRNA design algorithms for improving accuracy. (iii) CRISPRoffT offers potential off-target overlap and comparative analysis of each guide sequence under different technologies and conditions. (iv) CRISPRoffT provides comparative analyses of validated off-targets of each guide sequence under different technologies and conditions. (v) CRISPRoffT offers the on-target site status of each gene under different technologies and conditions. (vi) Additionally, the comparative analysis of the number of potential off-targets and ratio of validated off-targets for different on-target sites under various conditions was performed in 34 cell lines or cell types or tissues, 29 technologies and 84 Cas enzyme sgRNA combinations. Overall, CRISPRoffT is a unique and comprehensive resource for CRISPR/Cas-related research, and it is expected to greatly benefit the CRISPR geneediting therapies.

In order to maintain CRISPRoffT as a cutting-edge CRISPR/Cas off-target database, we will continuously collect and update new data every 6 months. As more and more studies are conducted to develop and screen for more potent Cas9/12 homologs and Cas enzymes, we will continue to collect available data. CRISPR technology is increasingly being used for a wide range of applications [\(65\)](#page-10-0), including not only DNA editing, but also RNA editing [\(66\)](#page-10-0) and epigenome editing [\(54\)](#page-10-0). This will be a direction for further expansion of our database. Additionally, though our current database focuses on therapeutic CRISPR applications in human cell lines and mouse models, we will cover a wider range of species in the next version of CRISPRoffT to further benefit the various research directions, such as nonhuman primates [\(67\)](#page-10-0), nematodes [\(68\)](#page-10-0), fruit flies [\(69\)](#page-10-0), zebrafish [\(70\)](#page-10-0) and others. Furthermore, we welcome users to share publicly available offtarget data on our website. We will develop Natural Language Processing methods to query and organize published studies, develop off-target prediction algorithms and guide RNA design algorithms based on validated off-target data in our database. This database could aid researchers in selecting the optimal technology and contribute to the development of more sensitive technologies. CRISPRoffT will also facilitate extensive research based on CRISPR/Cas gene editing and advances in CRISPR gene-editing therapies. We firmly believe that CRISPRoffT will serve as a valuable resource for the safety-driven design of CRISPR-based therapeutics.
